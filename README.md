## The entry (122 characters)

> Hey, it's you from 2042. Delete that tweet. Not the mean one — the boring one. It's the one they read out at your funeral.

Future-you urgently warns present-you to delete a tweet — not the mean one, the boring one — because that is the one they read out at your funeral. The misdirection is the twist, and the funeral detail is what makes the warning feel like it comes from someone who has already seen how the story ends. The whole joke arrives inside the text itself, with no setup and no context, in the voice of a future self who is embarrassed on your behalf.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python standard library only, no network, no local imports) writes the single line above to `/output/entry.txt` — exactly 122 characters, UTF-8, no trailing newline.

## Run

```sh
python3 -P -s main.py
```

The entry is written to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` when set).
